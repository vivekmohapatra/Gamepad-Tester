      Reading stick drift

      Drift is when a stick reports movement while you are not touching it. Release both sticks and watch the resting figures above. Under 0.05 is normal wear. A reading that climbs on its own, or sits above your deadzone, means the potentiometer or hall sensor is worn and games will creep without input.

      What a deadzone does

      The deadzone is the slack a game ignores around centre so small imperfections do not move your character. Raising it hides mild drift at the cost of fine aim. Use the slider to find the smallest value that keeps your resting dot inside the dashed circle.

      How to use the gamepad test

      The whole check takes about a minute. Work down the page and the diagnostics panel keeps score of what you have proved is working.

      1. Connect the controller

        Plug the pad in over USB, or pair it over Bluetooth in your system settings first. USB is the more reliable of the two for testing because it rules out pairing and battery problems.

        Browsers keep a controller hidden until it sends input, so press A or Cross once. The status light turns teal the moment the pad is seen.

      2. Press every button once

        Work around the pad: face buttons, both bumpers, both triggers, all four D-pad directions, View and Menu, and press both sticks in until they click.

        Each input lights orange on the diagram and ticks off a chip in Diagnostics. A chip that refuses to light is the fault you came here to find.

      3. Roll the sticks and pull the triggers

        Rotate each stick slowly through a full circle against the outer edge. The dot should trace a clean ring and reach the rim in every direction.

        Squeeze each trigger from rest to fully down. A healthy analog trigger sweeps smoothly from 0% to 100% without jumping or stopping short.

      4. Let go and read the drift figure

        Take your hands off the pad for a few seconds. The resting numbers settle and the panel tells you whether either stick is reporting movement on its own.

        Under 0.05 is normal wear. Higher than that is drift. Raise the deadzone slider until the resting dot sits inside the dashed circle to see how much slack a game would need to hide it.

      Questions people ask

      The questions that come up most often about controller faults, and what the readings on this page actually mean.

      Why is my controller not detected?

      Browsers deliberately hide a controller until it sends input, so press any button or move a stick after plugging it in. That single press is usually the whole fix.

      If it still does not appear: check the pad is paired in your operating system first, try a different USB port or a data-capable cable rather than a charge-only one, close Steam and other overlays that can grab the device exclusively, and make sure a wireless dongle is seated properly.

      How do I check for stick drift?

      Let go of both sticks and watch the resting deviation reading in the Diagnostics panel. A healthy stick settles under about 0.05.

      Anything that keeps moving on its own, or sits well above your deadzone, is drifting. In a game that shows up as your character walking or your aim sliding while you are not touching anything.

      Does this work with Xbox, PlayStation and Switch controllers?

      Yes. Any pad your operating system recognises reports through the browser Gamepad API over USB or Bluetooth, including Xbox Series and Xbox One pads, the DualSense and DualShock 4, the Switch Pro Controller, Joy-Cons, and third-party pads from 8BitDo, Logitech, Razer, PowerA and others.

      Pads that report the standard mapping are drawn on the diagram. Everything else falls back to a numbered readout, which still tests every input correctly.

      Why is the controller diagram greyed out?

      The diagram only animates for pads that report the browser's standard mapping. Some arcade sticks, flight controls, racing wheels and older or unusual pads report a custom layout instead, so the drawing would light the wrong parts.

      When that happens the diagram dims and the Buttons panel switches to raw indexes. Every input is still read and tested, just by number rather than by name.

      Why does the vibration test do nothing?

      Rumble needs three things at once: a pad with motors, a browser that exposes them, and a connection that carries them. Chrome and Edge support the dual-rumble effect; Firefox and Safari support is limited or absent.

      The Device panel tells you which side is missing. If it reads unavailable, the browser cannot see the motors at all. Trying the same pad over USB instead of Bluetooth often brings them back.

      My trigger stops short of 100%. Is that a fault?

      Usually yes, and it is a common one. Analog triggers use a sensor that wears, and dirt or a worn spring can stop the pull short. A trigger that reads 92% at full squeeze will feel unresponsive in racing and shooter games.

      A trigger that jumps between values rather than sweeping smoothly points at the same problem. Compare both triggers against each other on the meters before deciding.

      What is a deadzone and what should I set it to?

      A deadzone is the slack a game ignores around the centre of a stick so that tiny imperfections do not move your character. The slider here lets you see the effect.

      Raising it hides mild drift at the cost of fine aim, so find the smallest value that keeps the resting dot inside the dashed circle. If you need more than about 0.20, the stick is worn enough to be worth repairing.

      Can I fix stick drift myself?

      Sometimes. Drift is often dust or worn contacts in the potentiometer rather than a dead part, so compressed air around the stick base, or a proper contact cleaner applied by someone comfortable opening the pad, can clear it.

      If it returns, the stick module itself is worn. Replacement modules are cheap, and hall-effect sticks use magnets instead of contacts so they do not wear the same way. Check your warranty first, because opening the pad will usually void it.

      Which browsers support the controller test?

      Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Opera and other Chromium browsers all work well on Windows, macOS, Linux and ChromeOS. Safari supports the Gamepad API but lags on rumble and on some pad layouts.

      On Android, Chrome reads most Bluetooth pads. On iPhone and iPad, Safari reads MFi and modern console pads paired in system settings.

      Is any of my input recorded?

      No. Everything on this page is computed in your browser from the live Gamepad API and then discarded. No button presses, stick positions or device names are sent to a server, stored, or shared. Closing the tab ends it completely.

      Reading the level meter

      0 dBFS is the loudest a digital signal can go, so every reading here is a negative number counting down from that ceiling. Speaking normally, you want peaks landing between -12 and -6 dBFS, which leaves headroom for a laugh without touching the limit.

      Why raw input matters

      Browsers apply echo cancelling, noise removal and automatic gain by default. That helps on calls and hides real faults. Switch to raw input to hear the microphone itself, including the hum, hiss or crackle that processing was papering over.

      Distance beats equipment

      Moving twice as far from a microphone costs you roughly 6 dB of signal while the room noise stays where it was. A cheap microphone a hand's width away will out-perform an expensive one across the desk, every time.

      How to use the microphone test

      Give the page permission once, watch the meter while you talk, then record a sample so you hear what everyone else hears.

      1. Allow the microphone

        Press Start microphone. Your browser asks for permission, and the recording indicator in the tab or address bar confirms it is live.

        If you have more than one input, pick the right one from the device list. The list only fills in with real names after permission is granted.

      2. Talk and watch the level

        Speak normally at your usual distance. The waveform should move clearly and the peak reading should land somewhere around -12 dBFS to -6 dBFS.

        Flat line means nothing is arriving. A meter pinned at 0 dB means you are clipping, which is the distortion people hear as crackling on calls.

      3. Record and listen back

        Stop talking for a moment to let the noise floor settle, then record a few seconds and play it back. This is the only reliable way to judge hiss, echo and room noise.

        If the playback sounds distant or hollow, move closer, or check you are not recording from a laptop mic while wearing a headset.

      Questions people ask

      What the numbers mean, and what to try when the meter stays flat.

      Why does the page ask for permission?

      Browsers will not open a microphone without an explicit prompt, and that prompt is the only way this page can read audio at all. Permission applies to this tab only and ends when you close it or press stop.

      If you dismissed the prompt by accident, click the padlock or camera icon in the address bar and reset the microphone permission for this site, then reload.

      My microphone shows a flat line. What now?

      Work through these in order:

      • Check the right device is selected in the list, not a disconnected headset or a virtual cable.
      • Check the physical mute switch on your headset, and the mute key on your keyboard.
      • On Windows, open Settings, Privacy and security, Microphone, and confirm your browser is allowed.
      • On macOS, open System Settings, Privacy and Security, Microphone, and do the same.
      • Close Zoom, Teams, Discord and OBS, which can hold the device exclusively.

      What microphone level should I aim for?

      Speaking normally, peaks around -12 dBFS to -6 dBFS give you clear speech with room left for a laugh or a raised voice. Average level sitting near -18 dBFS is healthy.

      If peaks hit 0 dB the signal is clipping and permanently distorted, so turn the input gain down. If you cannot get above about -30 dBFS, turn gain up or move closer.

      What is a noise floor and what is a good one?

      The noise floor is the level of your room and equipment when nobody is speaking: fans, traffic, air conditioning, and hiss from the microphone itself.

      Below about -60 dBFS is quiet enough that nobody will notice. Around -50 dBFS is audible on a call. Above -40 dBFS people will hear your room before they hear you, and no amount of software noise removal fixes that cleanly.

      Can I test a USB, headset or Bluetooth microphone?

      Yes. Anything your operating system lists as an input appears in the device list, including USB condenser mics, headset booms, webcam mics, audio interfaces and Bluetooth earbuds.

      Choose the device before you start, or switch it mid-test and the page will restart the stream on the new input.

      Why does my Bluetooth headset sound terrible when the mic is on?

      Most Bluetooth headsets cannot send and receive high quality audio at the same time. The moment the microphone opens, the connection drops to a narrow voice profile, music quality collapses and everything sounds thin and muffled.

      That is a limitation of the Bluetooth profile rather than a fault. A USB or wired headset avoids it entirely, which is why it is the usual recommendation for calls and recording.

      Why does my voice sound quiet even at full volume?

      Automatic gain control is the usual cause. Browsers apply it by default to keep call levels even, and it fights any gain you set yourself.

      Distance is the other cause, and it matters more than people expect: doubling your distance from the mic cuts the level by roughly 6 dB. A microphone a hand's width from your mouth will always beat one across the desk.

      Is my recording uploaded anywhere?

      No. The waveform, the meters and the recording all stay in your browser's memory. Nothing is written to a server, nothing is stored on disk unless you save it yourself, and reloading the page discards it.

      Which browsers can test a microphone?

      Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Opera all support microphone access on desktop, and Chrome and Safari support it on Android and iOS.

      Access requires a secure connection, so a page served over plain HTTP cannot open your microphone at all. That is a browser rule rather than a site setting.

      Does this test whether other people can hear me?

      It tests everything up to the moment your voice leaves the browser: the device, the driver, the level and the sound quality. If the waveform moves and the playback sounds right, your microphone is working.

      If it works here but not in a specific app, the fault is that app's device selection or permissions rather than your hardware, which is a much quicker thing to fix.

      Protect your hearing

      A sine wave carries far more continuous energy than music at the same meter reading, and it is much easier to misjudge. Start with the volume slider low, raise it slowly, and never begin a sweep with headphones already on your ears.

      What a swapped pair sounds like

      Reversed stereo is surprisingly hard to notice and quietly ruins every film, game and recording on that setup. The left and right buttons here are the fastest way to catch it, and the fix is usually one cable swapped at the back.

      Rattles are not your ears

      If the sweep produces a buzz or rattle at one particular frequency and then cleans up again, something physical is resonating: a loose driver, a panel, or an object on the shelf. Note the frequency and run the sweep again to confirm.

      How to use the speaker test

      Start quiet. Test tones carry far more energy than music at the same meter reading, and a sine wave at high volume is unpleasant and can damage tweeters.

      1. Turn the volume down first

        Set your system volume low before you play anything, then raise it into the test. Pure tones sound much louder than music at the same level and there is no warning.

        Take headphones off your ears for the first press. If something is wrong with the balance or the output device, you find out safely.

      2. Check left, then right

        Play the left channel on its own and confirm sound comes only from the left. Repeat on the right, then play both and listen for a centred, even image.

        Sound from the wrong side means a swapped cable or a reversed connector. Sound from both when only one is playing means your output is summing to mono.

      3. Sweep the range

        Run the sweep and note where you stop hearing the tone at each end. Most adults stop somewhere between 14 kHz and 17 kHz, and that upper limit falls with age.

        Buzzing, rattling or a sudden drop partway through points at the speaker rather than your ears. Note the frequency and try the same sweep on headphones to confirm.

      Questions people ask

      Channel problems, hearing range, and what the sweep is actually telling you.

      How do I test left and right speakers?

      Use the channel buttons. Each one plays a short burst on one side only, so you can confirm which speaker is which and that both are alive.

      The most common finding is a stereo pair wired the wrong way round, which is invisible until you test it and quietly ruins every film and game you have played on that setup.

      Why can I only hear one side?

      Check in this order: the balance control in your operating system sound settings, the cable seated fully at both ends, a different port or device, and a different pair of headphones in the same port.

      If one side is dead on every device, the output is at fault. If it follows the headphones to another device, the headphones are at fault. That single swap tells you which thing to replace.

      What is the frequency sweep for?

      It plays a continuous tone that climbs from a deep bass note to the top of the audible range, so you hear your whole system in one pass.

      It shows two separate things: where your hearing stops, and where your speakers stop. A rattle at a particular frequency is a loose driver or cabinet. A clean fade out at the top is normal for both ears and equipment.

      Why can't I hear the highest frequencies?

      Almost certainly age, and it is completely normal. Human hearing is often quoted as 20 Hz to 20 kHz, but that upper figure describes healthy young ears. Most people in their thirties top out around 15 to 16 kHz and it keeps falling.

      Small speakers and phone speakers also roll off early, so if you cannot hear 60 Hz on a laptop that is the laptop rather than your ears.

      Is it safe to play test tones through my speakers?

      At sensible volume, yes. The risk is at high volume: a sustained sine wave puts far more continuous energy into a driver than music does, and tweeters are the first thing to fail.

      Keep the level moderate, keep sustained tones short, and never start a sweep with headphones already on your ears.

      Can I test headphones, earbuds and Bluetooth devices?

      Yes, all of them. The test plays through whatever your system currently uses for output, so select your headphones as the default device first.

      Bluetooth adds noticeable delay between pressing a button and hearing the tone. That lag is the wireless link, not a fault, and it does not affect the channel or frequency results.

      Can I choose which output device the test uses?

      Where the browser allows it, yes, and an output picker appears. Chrome and Edge support choosing an output device from a web page; Firefox and Safari generally do not.

      When no picker is available, change your default output in system sound settings and the test follows it.

      What causes crackling or popping during the test?

      Crackling that appears at every frequency usually means a buffer problem on the computer rather than the speaker: a busy CPU, an aggressive power saving setting, or a driver that needs updating.

      Crackling that only appears around certain frequencies, or that you can make worse by touching the cable, is physical. A damaged cable, a loose connector or a torn driver all sound like that.

      Why is there a delay before I hear anything?

      Browsers suspend audio until you interact with the page, so the first press both starts the audio engine and plays the sound. Later presses respond immediately.

      Bluetooth output adds its own delay on top, typically between 100 and 300 milliseconds depending on the codec.

      Does this test speaker quality or just whether they work?

      It tests function rather than fidelity: which channel is which, whether both work, whether anything rattles, and how far the system reaches at each end of the range.

      Judging tonal quality needs a measurement microphone and a treated room. What this page reliably catches is the faults that matter: a dead side, a swapped pair, a buzzing driver, or a device that quietly collapsed to mono.

      Code, key and keyCode

      The physical code describes where a key sits and never changes with your language layout. The key is the character that position currently produces, which does. If a key lights here but types the wrong letter, your layout setting is the culprit, not the hardware.

      Ghosting explained

      Cheap keyboards wire their switches into a shared grid to save on components. Certain three-key combinations become ambiguous in that grid, so the keyboard drops the third press rather than guess. That is ghosting, and it is a design limit rather than a fault.

      Keys no browser can see

      Fn is resolved inside the keyboard itself and never reaches the computer. Alt+Tab, Ctrl+Alt+Delete and the Windows key are claimed by the operating system before any page sees them. None of those showing up here is normal and expected.

      How to use the keyboard test

      Capture mode keeps browser shortcuts from firing while you test. Press Escape twice at any point to release it.

      1. Click the layout, then start pressing

        Click once anywhere on the keyboard drawing so the page has focus. Keys light orange as you hold them and stay marked teal once proven.

        Work in rows rather than hunting: top row across, then each row down. That way you notice the gap rather than forgetting which keys you already did.

      2. Watch the live event readout

        Every press shows its physical code, the key character it produces, and whether the event is a repeat. That distinction matters: a key can be physically fine but mapped wrong by your layout.

        If a key produces the wrong character, the fault is your keyboard layout setting rather than the hardware.

        Gaming keyboards with N-key rollover keep counting well past six. Try WASD plus space plus shift, the combination that actually matters in games.

      Questions people ask

      Dead keys, double typing, ghosting, and the keys a browser is never allowed to see.

      Why is one of my keys not registering?

      If a key never lights here, the signal is not reaching the browser at all. Try it in a text editor to confirm, then unplug and reconnect the keyboard, try a different USB port, and test on another computer if you can.

      A single dead key on a mechanical keyboard is often a failed switch, which is replaceable. On a membrane or laptop keyboard it usually means debris under the key or a damaged membrane sheet, and a liquid spill is the usual cause.

      What is key ghosting and N-key rollover?

      Ghosting is when a keyboard stops registering additional keys because of how its switches are wired into a shared matrix. Press three keys in the wrong arrangement and the third simply never arrives.

      N-key rollover means every key is read independently, so any number can register at once. Hold several keys here and watch the counter to see which kind you own. Six is a common limit on USB keyboards and is plenty for most use.

      How do I test for a chattering or double-typing key?

      Chatter is a worn switch that sends two presses from one push. Press the suspect key slowly and deliberately about twenty times and watch the press counter for that key.

      If the count climbs faster than you pressed, the switch is bouncing. On a hot-swappable keyboard you can replace the switch; otherwise some keyboards let you raise the debounce time in their configuration software.

      Why do browser shortcuts still fire sometimes?

      Capture mode stops most keys reaching the browser, but a handful are reserved by the operating system and can never be intercepted by a web page. That is a security boundary rather than a limitation of this test.

      Ctrl+W, Alt+Tab, Ctrl+Alt+Delete, Command+Q and the Windows key are the usual ones. Test those in your own operating system rather than in a browser tab.

      Why don't Fn, media keys or the power button show up?

      The Fn key is handled inside the keyboard itself and never sends a key code to the computer, so no software on any platform can see it. It is not broken.

      Media keys and brightness keys often go straight to the operating system too. If volume actually changes when you press them, they work, even though nothing lights up here.

      Can I test a laptop keyboard?

      Yes, and it works the same way. The layout drawn here is a full-size one, so a laptop without a number pad will simply leave that block unmarked, which is expected.

      If several neighbouring keys are dead on a laptop, suspect the ribbon cable or a spill rather than individual switches.

      What do code, key and keyCode mean?

      code is the physical position on the keyboard and does not change with your language layout. key is the character that position currently produces, which does.

      So on a French AZERTY layout the key next to Tab reports a code of KeyQ but a key of 'a'. keyCode is an older number that browsers still report for compatibility.

      Can I test a keyboard on a phone or tablet?

      Only a physical one. On-screen touch keyboards send text rather than key events, so most of what this page measures does not apply to them.

      A Bluetooth or USB keyboard attached to a phone or tablet does work, and reports the same codes it would on a desktop.

      Does the test record what I type?

      No. Key events are read, drawn on screen and discarded. Nothing is sent anywhere, nothing is stored, and reloading the page clears every mark.

      For obvious reasons, do not type passwords into any keyboard test, here or anywhere else. Test with ordinary letters.

      My key repeats on its own. What causes that?

      A key that fires continuously without being held usually has something physically holding the switch down: debris, a sticky spill, or a keycap fouling its housing. Pull the cap and check underneath.

      If nothing is stuck, the switch has failed closed and needs replacing. In the meantime most operating systems let you remap the key to nothing so it stops interrupting you.

      How to use the mouse test

      Right-click and middle-click menus are suppressed inside the test pad so the buttons can be read properly. Everywhere else on the page behaves normally.

        Move into the test pad and click left, right and middle, then press the thumb buttons if your mouse has them. Each one lights on the diagram and ticks off in the tally.

        A button that clicks physically but never lights is a failed switch or a broken solder joint, and on side buttons it is often just a driver profile that has unbound them.

        Roll the wheel slowly in both directions, then press it in for middle click and tilt it sideways if it tilts. Each notch shows up as a step.

        Click the same button twenty times, deliberately and slowly. The chatter check flags any pair of clicks arriving too close together to be human.

        Then sweep the mouse steadily across the pad to measure polling rate. Higher is smoother, but what matters most is that it stays steady rather than collapsing.

      Questions people ask

      Double clicks you did not make, side buttons that ignore you, and what polling rate really tells you.

      What is double-click chatter and how do I test for it?

      Chatter is a worn or dirty switch sending two clicks from one press. It is the single most common mouse fault, and it shows up as files opening when you meant to select them, or dragging that drops halfway.

      Click one button twenty times slowly and watch the chatter counter. Any interval under about 50 milliseconds is faster than a human can click, so each one flagged is the switch misbehaving rather than you.

      Can I fix a mouse that double clicks?

      Often, yes. Many switches fail because of oxidation on the contacts rather than mechanical wear, and working the button hard a few hundred times sometimes clears it temporarily. Contact cleaner applied inside the switch works better and lasts longer.

      The permanent fix is replacing the switch, which is a cheap part but needs soldering. Check the warranty first, because several manufacturers cover this fault for years and it is a known defect on some popular models.

      Why don't my side buttons register?

      The usual cause is software rather than hardware. Manufacturer utilities from Logitech, Razer, Corsair and others often bind side buttons to macros or profile switching, and those never reach the browser as ordinary button events.

      Set the buttons back to their default forward and back actions in that software and retest. If they still do nothing here but work elsewhere, the browser is filtering them; if they do nothing anywhere, the switch has failed.

      What is polling rate and does a higher one matter?

      Polling rate is how often the mouse reports its position, measured in Hz. 125 Hz means eight milliseconds between updates; 1000 Hz means one.

      Above about 500 Hz the returns diminish sharply for most people. What matters more is consistency: a rate that swings wildly points at a failing cable, a congested USB hub, or wireless interference.

      How accurate is the polling rate shown here?

      Treat it as an indication rather than a measurement. Browsers coalesce mouse events and tie them to the display refresh, so a 1000 Hz mouse often reports lower here even when it is working perfectly.

      Use it to compare a mouse against itself over time, or to spot a rate that collapses during movement. For an exact figure, use a dedicated desktop utility.

      Does this test measure DPI or sensor accuracy?

      No, and no web page can measure DPI reliably. The browser reports movement in CSS pixels after the operating system has already applied pointer acceleration and display scaling, so the raw sensor counts are gone before they arrive.

      The movement trace is still useful for spotting jitter, stutter and a sensor that loses tracking on a reflective surface.

      Why doesn't the right-click menu appear?

      It is suppressed inside the test pad on purpose, because the context menu would open over the diagram and swallow the button event before it could be read.

      Right-click works normally everywhere else on the page. Move the pointer outside the pad if you need the menu.

      Can I test a trackpad or a trackball?

      Yes. Both report the same events as a mouse, so buttons, scrolling and movement all register. Trackpad gestures arrive as scroll events, which is why two-finger scrolling shows up here.

      Polling rate is not meaningful on a trackpad, since the numbers reflect the gesture engine rather than a sensor.

      My scroll wheel skips or jumps backwards.

      That is a worn optical encoder inside the wheel, and it is a common failure. Dust on the encoder wheel gives the same symptom and is worth ruling out first with compressed air.

      Some mice have a free-spin mode that disengages the notched mechanism, so check you have not toggled it accidentally before assuming a fault.

      Is my pointer movement being tracked?

      Only inside this page, only while it is open, and only to draw on screen. No coordinates, clicks or timings leave your browser, and nothing is stored.

      How to use the webcam test

      Questions people ask

      Why is my webcam preview black?

      In order of likelihood: a privacy shutter or sliding cover still closed, another application holding the camera, the camera disabled in system privacy settings, or the wrong device selected in the list.

      If the camera's indicator light is on but the picture is black, the shutter is the answer nine times out of ten.

      Another app is using my camera. How do I release it?

      Most operating systems allow only one application to hold a camera at a time. Fully quit Zoom, Teams, Skype, Discord and OBS rather than just closing their windows, because several keep running in the system tray.

      On Windows, check Task Manager for background instances. A reboot clears any stubborn case in a few seconds.

      Why is my resolution lower than the camera supports?

      Browsers negotiate a stream rather than demanding the maximum. Bandwidth, lighting, USB port speed and the camera's own frame rate rules all push the negotiated resolution down.

      A 1080p webcam on a USB 2.0 hub shared with other devices will often settle at 720p. Plugging directly into the computer usually restores it.

      How do I switch between front and rear cameras?

      Use the camera list, which fills in with real device names once permission is granted. On a phone, the front and rear cameras appear as separate entries.

      Some phones expose several rear lenses, including ultrawide and telephoto, so you may see more entries than you expected.

      Does this record or upload my video?

      No. The video stream is displayed directly in the page and never leaves your device. Snapshots are drawn locally and are only saved if you choose to download one.

      Pressing stop, or closing the tab, ends the stream and releases the camera.

      Why is my frame rate low?

      Light, almost always. In dim conditions a camera holds the shutter open longer for each frame, and a longer exposure means fewer frames per second. A 30 fps webcam routinely drops to 15 fps in a dark room.

      A window or a lamp in front of you fixes it instantly and improves the picture more than a more expensive camera would.

      Can I test a virtual camera from OBS or Snap?

      Yes. Virtual cameras appear in the device list alongside physical ones, so you can confirm your scene is being output correctly before joining a meeting.

      If a virtual camera is missing, its host application usually needs to be running and the virtual camera explicitly started.

      The image is upside down or the wrong way round.

      A mirrored preview is deliberate and normal. It feels natural to you, and video call apps do the same thing, while other participants see the unmirrored image. Toggle the mirror control to see what they see.

      A genuinely upside-down picture is a driver or mounting issue, common on laptops where the module is installed inverted. The manufacturer's camera utility usually has a flip setting.

      Which browsers support the webcam test?

      Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Opera all support camera access on desktop, and Chrome and Safari support it on Android and iOS.

      Camera access requires a secure connection. A page served over plain HTTP cannot open a camera at all, on any browser.

      Does my camera work but look grainy and washed out?

      Light in front of you rather than behind you, and avoid sitting with a bright window at your back. Almost every bad webcam picture is a lighting problem.

      Test it the week it arrives

      Manufacturers publish defect policies, and many will not treat a panel as faulty until several dead sub-pixels are present or one sits in the central zone. Return windows are short, so check a new monitor or laptop on day one and photograph anything you find.

      Bleed against glow

      Move your head. Backlight bleed stays exactly where it is; IPS glow shifts and fades as your viewing angle changes. That one test separates a possible warranty claim from a normal property of the panel technology you bought.

      Judge it in the room you use

      Face-on in a blacked-out room, every LCD looks bad. If you only notice bleed while hunting for it, it is normal. If it interrupts you during an ordinary dark scene at your usual brightness, it is excessive.

      How to use the screen test

      Clean the screen first. Most suspected dead pixels turn out to be dust, and you will not tell the difference until the screen is a solid colour.

      1. Clean the screen and dim the room

        Wipe the panel with a dry microfibre cloth. A speck of dust on black looks exactly like a stuck white pixel until you touch it.

        Turn the lights down. Backlight bleed and clouding are invisible in a bright room and obvious in a dark one.

      2. Cycle every colour field

        Go full screen and step through black, white, red, green and blue. Look across the whole panel each time rather than staring at the centre.

        A pixel that stays black on white is dead. One that stays red, green or blue on every field is stuck. Dead is permanent; stuck sometimes recovers.

      3. Check gradients and refresh rate

        The gradient field reveals banding: visible steps instead of a smooth fade, which points at an 8-bit panel, a low colour depth setting, or an aggressive cable.

        The refresh reading shows what your browser is actually getting. If you paid for 144 Hz and see 60, check the display settings and the cable before anything else.

      Questions people ask

      Dead versus stuck pixels, bleed versus glow, and how much of either is normal.

      What is the difference between a dead pixel and a stuck pixel?

      A dead pixel receives no power and stays black on every colour, including a full white field. A stuck pixel is permanently lit in one colour, so it shows as a bright red, green, blue or white dot against black.

      The distinction matters because stuck pixels sometimes recover on their own or with a flashing pattern, while dead pixels effectively never do.

      Can this fix a stuck pixel?

      Sometimes. The flashing pattern rapidly cycles colours to exercise a sub-pixel that has got lodged, and it does occasionally free one. Leave it running over the affected area for ten to thirty minutes.

      It is not guaranteed and it does nothing for a genuinely dead pixel. If the flashing is uncomfortable to watch, look away, and skip it entirely if you are sensitive to flashing light.

      How many dead pixels are normal on a new screen?

      More than most people expect. Manufacturers work to published defect policies, and many will not consider a panel faulty until several dead sub-pixels are present, or one is in the central zone.

      Test a new monitor or laptop as soon as it arrives and photograph anything you find, because return windows are short and the evidence is easier to gather on day one.

      What is backlight bleed and is mine too much?

      Backlight bleed is light escaping around the edges of an LCD, visible as brighter patches on a black field in a dark room. It is a byproduct of how LCDs are built and essentially every LCD has some.

      Judge it in normal viewing rather than face-on in a blacked-out room, where every panel looks bad. If you notice it during ordinary use, it is excessive; if you can only find it while hunting for it, it is normal.

      What is IPS glow and how is it different?

      IPS glow is a silvery haze in the corners that shifts as you move your head, and it is inherent to the IPS panel type rather than a defect. Backlight bleed stays put when you move.

      That movement test is the quickest way to tell them apart, and it matters because glow is not covered by any warranty while severe bleed sometimes is.

      What causes colour banding on the gradient?

      Visible steps instead of a smooth fade usually mean the panel or the pipeline is working at a lower colour depth than the image needs. Many panels are 8-bit or 6-bit with dithering.

      Before assuming the panel, check the colour depth setting in your graphics control panel, and check the cable: an under-specified HDMI or DisplayPort cable at high resolution and refresh rate will reduce depth to keep up.

      Is the refresh rate reading accurate?

      It measures how often the browser can actually paint, which normally matches your display's refresh rate closely. It is a good check of whether a high refresh monitor is genuinely running at its rated speed.

      Variable refresh rate, battery saving modes and a busy machine can all pull the reading below the panel's maximum, so take a reading with the system otherwise idle.

      Can I test for OLED burn-in with this?

      Yes, and the solid colour fields are the right tool. Burn-in appears as a faint permanent ghost of something previously shown, usually a taskbar, a logo or a heads-up display, and it is most visible on a mid-grey or a solid colour field.

      Uniform full-screen colour also drives most OLED panels' own pixel refresh routines, which is a reasonable maintenance habit for a screen that shows static content.

      Does the test work on a phone, tablet or TV?

      Yes. Full-screen mode works on most mobile browsers, though iOS Safari restricts it in places, in which case scroll the page so the colour field fills the display.

      On a TV, open the page in the built-in browser or cast a laptop to it. Turn off any motion smoothing and dynamic contrast processing first, because both alter what you are trying to measure.

      Why should I test in a dark room?

      Ambient light washes out exactly the faults you are looking for. Backlight bleed, clouding, black uniformity and faint stuck pixels are all low-contrast details that room light hides completely.

      Bright fields are the exception: check white and light grey in normal lighting, since that is where dead pixels and dust show best.
